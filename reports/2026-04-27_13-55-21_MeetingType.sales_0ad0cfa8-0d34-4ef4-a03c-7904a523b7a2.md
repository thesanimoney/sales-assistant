## Implication Questions — 3/10
Questions asked to develop the consequences and urgency of the prospect's problems.

### Implication questions asked
- "How many hours, how much resource are you losing because you don't have this?"
- "So you're talking about time loss — over the course of a year, roughly 3-4 months of inefficient resource usage?"

# 4. OBJECTIONS ANALYSIS

## Objection 1: "Do you have ready-made solutions... or do you start completely from scratch every time?"
- **Type:** Implied objection about speed, risk, and cost.
- **How it was handled:** Alexander answered reasonably well: "We have a lot of accumulated work — we have boxes... a semi-ready solution that we specifically finalize for you."
- **How it SHOULD have been handled:** Good start, but incomplete. He should have tied it to reduced implementation risk and timeline.
- **Better language:** "We don't start from a blank page. We reuse proven modules for sourcing, candidate processing, workflow logic, and AI evaluation, then tailor them to your process. That means lower risk and faster delivery than pure custom development, while avoiding the rigidity of SaaS. On the next call I'll show you exactly which parts are already proven and which would be customized for Okko."

## Objection 2: "Will there be an avatar... or is it still voice-only?"
- **Type:** Product maturity objection / readiness concern.
- **How it was handled:** The rep said MVP is voice-only and avatar is on roadmap in about a month.
- **How it SHOULD have been handled:** He answered honestly, which is good. But he failed to anchor whether avatar is mission-critical or cosmetic.
- **Better language:** "Today, voice is production-ready; avatar is in roadmap. The more important question is whether avatar is actually required for your use case or whether consistent screening quality matters more in phase one. If avatar materially affects candidate engagement for you, we'll include it in scope planning and show what's already tested versus what's production-ready."

## Objection 3: Price model comparison with competitors
Prospect said: "Someone charges 50 cents per conversation for voice, another charges 20 cents for voice plus avatar."
- **Type:** Implied objection about commercial model and cost benchmarking.
- **How it was handled:** Alexander shifted to ownership model: "You own the solution... You don't pay us ongoing fees..."
- **How it SHOULD have been handled:** He reframed, but skipped the economic comparison. He should have separated build-vs-buy economics.
- **Better language:** "Those per-conversation prices make sense in SaaS models. Our model is different: upfront build and ownership. The right choice depends on your volume, need for customization, and long-term total cost. On the next call we should compare both paths against your expected usage so you can decide based on economics, not just sticker price."

## Objection 4: Need to see practical proof before moving forward
Prospect said: "Can we at the next call see something practical...?"
- **Type:** Trust / proof objection.
- **How it was handled:** Accepted and scheduled a meeting.
- **How it SHOULD have been handled:** Fine, but weakly. The rep should have defined what will be shown and what information is needed from the prospect first.
- **Better language:** "Yes. We'll tailor that session around your recruiting workflow. If you send a light requirements brief beforehand, we'll show the closest relevant cases, sample interfaces, and a draft phase-one architecture so the meeting answers your internal committee questions, not just gives a generic demo."

## Objection 5: SAP may be expensive and entrenched
Prospect implied dissatisfaction but not explicit resistance to switching.
- **Type:** Change management / replacement risk.
- **How it was handled:** Mostly via anti-SaaS commentary and custom flexibility claims.
- **How it SHOULD have been handled:** The rep should have acknowledged migration and coexistence risk.
- **Better language:** "We don't need to frame this as a big-bang replacement on day one. We can assess whether phase one should automate the recruiting workflow around SAP, integrate into it, or replace only the highest-friction pieces first. That reduces implementation risk."
