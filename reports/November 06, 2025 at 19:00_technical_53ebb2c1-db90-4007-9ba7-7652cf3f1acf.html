Objective
Diagnose why production false positives are far above benchmark, agree on immediate production mitigations, and define an evaluation plan for a structural fix.

✗ What Went Wrong
The team admitted they promoted a fine-tuned model without clinical held-out review.
"We didn't have a held-out clinical review at the time. We trusted the eval loss."
Why it matters: This is a major process failure. In a clinical workflow, loss curves are not an acceptable proxy for grounded extraction quality or patient-safety-related error modes.
They changed the model and criterion set at the same time, making causal diagnosis harder.
"We didn't measure it cleanly because we changed the criterion set at the same time, so it's confounded."
Why it matters: This weakens confidence in attributing the regression to the model swap and indicates poor release/change isolation.
The original fine-tuning objective was misaligned with the real task.
"The labels included the criterion ID and a binary eligible-or-not flag. They did not include text spans pointing at the supporting evidence."
Why it matters: This setup encouraged shortcut learning and made hallucinated findings a foreseeable outcome, especially in extraction-like tasks requiring evidence grounding.
Ihor initially proposed an incomplete mitigation without accounting for Swedish-language negation constraints.
"Negation we can probably handle with a dedicated negation detection step using one of the medical NLP libraries — NegSpacy or scispaCy."
Why it matters: This was quickly corrected, but it shows early solutioning before validating language coverage for the dominant data distribution.
The team committed to end-of-week delivery without fully discussing validation, rollback, or load testing.
"End of week one for both."
Why it matters: The timeline may be achievable, but in a regulated/clinical-adjacent workflow, technical readiness is not enough; deployment safeguards and objective release criteria are required.
