## 3. SPIN SELLING ANALYSIS

### Situation Questions — 6/10
Questions asked to understand the prospect's current state, processes, and context.
- **Quoted situation questions asked:**
  - “Just to confirm, you're currently heading up the data engineering team at Acme?”
  - “How long has this been going on?”
  - “I want to make sure I understand your decision process before we go deeper. Is it just you involved in evaluating this or are there others we'd need to bring in?”
  - “And are you currently talking to any other vendors or is this more exploratory at this stage?”
- **Were they efficient?** Mostly yes, but the first half of the call was still broad. The rep asked just enough to orient, but not enough to understand the actual environment, stack, scale, or current operating model.
- **What critical situation context was NOT established?**
  - Current data stack / tooling
  - Cloud platform and migration specifics
  - Team structure beyond six engineers
  - Volume, complexity, and criticality of pipelines
  - Who owns architecture vs ops vs analytics requirements
  - Existing internal remediation attempts

### Problem Questions — 9/10
Questions asked to surface difficulties, dissatisfactions, and problems the prospect has.
- **Quoted problem questions asked:**
  - “What's the main thing that prompted you to reach out to us now? What's going on?”
  - “When a pipeline breaks, what actually happens on the business side? Like what's the downstream impact?”
  - “Has anyone tried to quantify what that's costing you? Either in lost time or actual business impact?”
  - “What would make this an easy yes for you and your CTO?”
- **Did the rep uncover explicit problems or stop at vague dissatisfaction?** The rep got to explicit pain: recurring pipeline failures, firefighting, missed reporting, board reporting delays, and shadow analytics risk.
- **What problems were visible but never probed?**
  - Why the pipelines break every other week
  - Whether this is a migration issue, reliability issue, data modeling issue, or resourcing issue
  - Whether there are SLA breaches, customer-facing impacts, or compliance risks
  - Whether the team has tried internal fixes already and failed

### Implication Questions — 9/10
Questions asked to develop the consequences and urgency of the prospect's problems.
- **Quoted implication questions asked:**
  - “And beyond the cost, what happens if this continues for another six months? What's the risk to the business?”
- **This is where most reps fail.** This rep did not fail here. They pushed past annoyance into business risk, credibility loss, shadow analytics, and board-level consequences.
- **What implications were left unexplored?**
  - Attrition / burnout on the six-person team
  - Opportunity cost of delayed ML initiatives
  - Executive escalation risk if the problem worsens
  - Whether bad data is affecting decisions beyond reporting
  - Cost of future migration delay or rework
- **2–3 implication questions the rep SHOULD have asked:**
  - “If the team keeps spending 40% of its time firefighting, what initiatives won’t get shipped this quarter?”
  - “If finance and sales keep losing trust in the data, what does that do to adoption of your analytics stack?”
  - “If shadow analytics starts in the business, how hard is that to unwind later?”

### Need-Payoff Questions — 8/10
Questions asked to get the prospect to articulate the value of solving the problem themselves.
- **Quoted need-payoff questions asked:**
  - “If you could wave a magic wand and fix this, what would the ideal outcome look like for you personally?”
  - “So if we could help you get to a place where your pipelines are stable, your team is back to building, and you have capacity for the ML work — would that be worth exploring further?”
- **Did the prospect verbalize the benefit, or did the rep pitch it at them?** Mostly the prospect verbalized it. The rep then reflected it back well and confirmed value. Good.
- **2–3 need-payoff questions the rep SHOULD have asked:**
  - “If your team got back 40% of its time, what would you prioritize first?”
  - “What would it mean to you if you could get through a week without firefighting or executive escalations?”
  - “How would a stable pipeline change the CTO’s appetite to invest in the broader data roadmap?”

## 5. MISSED QUESTIONS
- **“What does your current stack look like end to end?”**
  - **Type:** Situation
  - **Why it matters:** Would reveal complexity, integration points, and where the failures originate.
  - **Best moment:** Right after “We’re drowning.”

- **“What changed in the cloud migration that made this worse?”**
  - **Type:** Situation / Problem
  - **Why it matters:** Identifies root cause and scope; prevents a generic solution pitch.
  - **Best moment:** After “It got worse when we migrated to the new cloud setup.”

- **“Who owns the migration decisions and remediation budget today?”**
  - **Type:** MEDDIC (Economic Buyer / Decision Process)
  - **Why it matters:** Clarifies who can approve scope and whether the CTO is the true blocker.
  - **Best moment:** When discussing the CTO sign-off threshold.

- **“What exact criteria will your CTO use to judge this?”**
  - **Type:** MEDDIC (Decision Criteria)
  - **Why it matters:** Lets the rep tailor proof, timeline, and implementation plan to the real buying standard.
  - **Best moment:** Right after “he’d want to be involved.”

- **“What happens if you do nothing for another quarter?”**
  - **Type:** Implication
  - **Why it matters:** Quantifies urgency, risk, and likely internal escalation.
  - **Best moment:** After the prospect mentioned board reporting delay.

- **“Have you already tried to fix this internally? What worked, what didn’t?”**
  - **Type:** Problem
  - **Why it matters:** Reveals what the team has exhausted and whether they are looking for augmentation or replacement.
  - **Best moment:** After the eight-month timeline was mentioned.

- **“What’s the cost of the team’s burnout and attrition risk?”**
  - **Type:** Implication
  - **Why it matters:** The team’s morale is likely deteriorating; losing senior engineers would be costly.
  - **Best moment:** After “40% of their time on firefighting.”

- **“What would a successful engagement look like in 30/60/90 days?”**
  - **Type:** MEDDIC (Decision Criteria / Metrics)
  - **Why it matters:** Establishes measurable outcomes and helps avoid vague scope.
  - **Best moment:** Before proposing the technical discovery session.

- **“What’s the budget range you’d consider for a proper engagement?”**
  - **Type:** MEDDIC (Budget / Economic Buyer)
  - **Why it matters:** The rep learned the approval threshold but not the actual planned spend.
  - **Best moment:** Immediately after the $50K sign-off detail.

- **“What did the other firm pitch, and why wasn’t it a fit?”**
  - **Type:** Competitive / MEDDIC
  - **Why it matters:** Reveals competitor positioning and prospect preferences.
  - **Best moment:** When competition was mentioned.

## 6. OBJECTIONS ANALYSIS
- **Objection: “My CTO would need to sign off on anything above 50K.”**
  - **How it was handled:** The rep acknowledged it and moved to next steps.
  - **How it SHOULD have been handled:**
    - “Makes sense. What does your CTO care most about in deals like this — speed, risk reduction, or proof you’ve done it before?”
    - “What would he need to see for this to be a no-brainer?”

- **Objection: “We had one conversation with another firm but it wasn't a great fit, they were pushing a product we didn't need.”**
  - **How it was handled:** The rep noted it but did not explore it.
  - **How it SHOULD have been handled:**
    - “That’s useful. What specifically made it feel product-led instead of problem-led?”
    - “What should we do differently so this doesn’t feel like a product pitch?”

- **Objection / concern: “We can’t afford another failed migration.”**
  - **How it was handled:** The rep essentially accepted it and proposed discovery.
  - **How it SHOULD have been handled:**
    - “What failed the last time — scope, execution, stakeholder alignment, or technical approach?”
    - “What would have to be true for you to trust the next migration or remediation plan?”
